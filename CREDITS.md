## Demo sources

The hero chimera was generated by the project owner with Tripo. The owner authorized its inclusion in the demo and repository. It was edited and given a custom rig for Chimera. The input hero contains no animation clips.

The trebuchet comes from [Kenney's Castle Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/castle-kit), released under CC0. It was given a four-bone mechanical rig and reoriented for capture.

The instrumental demo soundtrack was generated with ACE-Step 1.5 through ComfyUI. It uses the existing `orchestral_22` take. Music generation is separate from Chimera's animation workflow.

## Models and software

- [ComfyUI](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI), the Comfy SDK and Comfy API proxy provide the workflow backend and app connection.
- [LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5) generates the motion video. [Qwen3.8-27B](https://huggingface.co/Comfy-Org/Qwen3.8-27B) rewrites prompts. [MODELS.md](MODELS.md) lists the files and terms.
- [SkinTokens](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/SkinTokens), MIT, creates a rig and skin weights for an unrigged model.
- [AllTracker](https://github.com/aharley/alltracker), MIT, tracks points through generated video.
- [SigLIP 2](https://huggingface.co/google/siglip2-so400m-patch14-384), Apache-2.0, estimates facing direction.
- [Blender](https://www.blender.org/) imports, renders and exports 3D assets.
- [model-viewer](https://modelviewer.dev/) displays GLB models and animations in the browser.

The fitting approach draws on the published idea of matching a skinned mesh to video through silhouette and point correspondences. Chimera's implementation is in `pipeline/fit.py` and `pipeline/rigtorch.py`.
